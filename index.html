01 / Selected research

Forecasts should survive contact with real time.

My work connects econometric theory, machine learning, and text-as-data with a consistent question: what information would truly have been available when a forecast was made?

01

Job Market Paper · 2026

New Evidence of the Usefulness of Real-Time Sentiment Indices in Econometric and Judgement-Based Forecasting Models

with A. Kabiri, John Landon-Lane, and Norman R. Swanson

EconBERTReal-time dataSPFMacroeconomic forecasting

Constructs three transformer-based narrative measures from U.S. economic and policy documents, then tests their marginal predictive content in real-time models and Survey of Professional Forecasters efficiency regressions.

31 / 33best-performing alternative specifications included at least one sentiment index.
Research contribution +

Narrative indices improve the econometric forecast set across target-variable and horizon combinations. SPF forecasts incorporate the new narrative measures, while widely used consumer-sentiment surveys still contain unexploited information.

02

Quantitative Economics · Revise & Resubmit

Consistent Factor Estimation and Forecasting in Factor-Augmented VAR Models

with John Chao, Yu Liu, and Norman R. Swanson

Factor modelsVariable selectionForecasting

Establishes when pre-screening irrelevant predictors restores consistent factor estimation—and consistent conditional-mean estimation—even when standard factor pervasiveness fails.

Research contribution +

The result gives data-rich forecasters a principled reason to screen predictors before extracting factors, expanding the settings in which FAVAR forecasts remain consistently estimable.

03

Working Paper · 2026

Diffusion Indices, Policy Rules, and LLM Narratives

Forecasting the U.S. Yield Curve in Real Time

Yield curveLLM narrativesMonetary policyRDNS

Runs a unified real-time horse race across macro diffusion indices, LLM narrative measures, and monetary-policy-rule information in dynamic Nelson–Siegel models.

Research contribution +

Variable-selection-based diffusion indices outperform conventional targeted-PCA alternatives; LLM indices add predictive information; and policy-rule components yield the largest improvements at the short end of the curve.

02 / EconBERT research

Economic direction,
not generic emotion.

General sentiment models often confuse cautious wording with bad economic news. EconBERT instead asks what a passage implies for macroeconomic conditions—so easing inflation pressure can be positive even when the prose sounds restrained.

01

Domain corpus

Federal Reserve, BLS, and Census narratives anchor the model in official economic language.

02

LLM-assisted labels

Pre-2013 passages receive positive, neutral, or negative macro-direction labels.

03

Distilled classifier

A compact DistilBERT student learns the task, then remains frozen out of sample.

04

Real-time index

Passage scores become source-balanced monthly measures suitable for forecast evaluation.
