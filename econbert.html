02 / EconBERT

Economic language.
Investment insight.

What is changing in the economy—and what deserves a closer look in markets? EconBERT turns economic text into structured measures of economic tone across growth, inflation, financial conditions, and risk. It gives quantitative researchers, investment teams, and portfolio leaders a consistent lens on the economic narrative, beyond whether a headline simply sounds optimistic or pessimistic.

01

Economic intelligence corpus

Official Fed, BLS, and Census narratives form the core. An expanding ProQuest news corpus brings higher-frequency reporting on markets, fiscal policy, and financial risk into the research agenda.

02

LLM-guided economic interpretation

An LLM teacher applies a macroeconomic rubric to label passages positive, neutral, or negative. The target is economic direction, not emotional wording: easing price pressure can signal improvement even in a cautious statement.

03

Teacher–student knowledge distillation

Teacher-generated labels transfer the task to a compact DistilBERT student. This label-based distillation makes repeated scoring practical; the research model is fitted on pre-2013 documents and then frozen for out-of-sample evaluation.

04

Narrative signals for nowcasting

Passage scores become source-balanced monthly tone indices. A complementary index combines eight economic themes. Paired with real-time data vintages, these let researchers test whether narratives add timely information to macro and market forecasts.

01 / Inflation and prices

Look beyond “prices rose.”

Inflation language connects household purchasing power, firms’ cost pressures, and the policy outlook. Economic context matters more than the emotional charge of a word.

Investment-research question

Are price pressures easing—or shifting between consumer demand, input costs, and margins?

Rates · Equities · Commodities

02 / Labor market

Read the balance of resilience and cooling.

Employment narratives connect hiring, labor supply, wages, and household income—dimensions that a single headline payroll number cannot fully describe.

Investment-research question

Is hiring moderating alongside resilient demand, or pointing to a broader slowdown?

Equities · Rates · Multi-asset

03 / Real activity

Follow the demand behind the cycle.

Language about production, orders, and business activity provides a narrative view of the real economy alongside released macroeconomic data.

Investment-research question

Is momentum broadening across the economy, or becoming concentrated in a few sectors?

Cyclical equities · Commodities · Macro

04 / Monetary policy

Put policy language in economic context.

Central-bank communication links the outlook for activity and inflation to policy decisions. Economic tone is distinct from a simple hawkish-versus-dovish label.

Investment-research question

How is the balance of growth and inflation concerns evolving around policy decisions?

Rates · FX · Equity duration

05 / Financial conditions

Track the language of credit transmission.

Banking and credit narratives describe how financing conditions reach households and firms, complementing market-based measures of financial stress.

Investment-research question

Are tighter lending conditions becoming a headwind for investment and demand?

Credit · Financial equities · Multi-asset

06 / Housing

Read a rate-sensitive part of the economy.

Housing narratives connect affordability, residential construction, and sales with the wider transmission of interest rates and household demand.

Investment-research question

Is the housing narrative signaling renewed demand or continued financing pressure?

Real estate · Homebuilders · Materials

07 / Consumption and retail sales

Connect the consumer to the growth outlook.

Retail and spending narratives add context to changes in household demand, connecting the macro outlook with questions about consumer-facing sectors.

Investment-research question

Is spending strength widespread, or masking softer demand in particular categories?

Consumer equities · Credit · Macro

08 / Uncertainty and downside risks

Distinguish caution from deterioration.

Risk language can reflect uncertainty about the outlook or evidence of weaker conditions. The distinction matters when interpreting cautious economic communication.

Investment-research question

Are downside concerns becoming broader or more economically specific?

Risk research · Credit · Multi-asset

Built for research, not just a score

An additional information set.
A testable investment question.

For a quant team, narrative indices can sit alongside structured data in a forecasting pipeline. For investment analysts and portfolio leaders, the eight themes provide a consistent way to organize questions about the cycle, policy, and risk.

The job market paper evaluates monthly indices in real-time nowcasting and forecasting exercises spanning macroeconomic activity, Treasury rates, equity returns, volatility, and credit conditions. Applications to individual sectors, FX, and commodities are research opportunities, not demonstrated trading results.
